Solve

Find the value (or values) of the unknown that make an equation or inequality true.

ExampleSolve 2x + 1 = 9
2x = 8, so x = 4

Equation

A statement that two expressions are equal. It always has an equals sign.

Example3x − 4 = 11

Linear

The highest power of the variable is 1. A linear equation gives a straight-line graph.

Example2x + 5 = 13 and y = 3x − 1 are linear.

Lowest common denominator (LCD)

The smallest number that all the denominators (bottoms of the fractions) divide into. Multiplying by it clears the fractions.

Examplex2 + x3 = 5
LCD = 6, so 3x + 2x = 30, x = 6

Inequality

A statement that two amounts are not equal, using <, >, ≤ or ≥. The answer is a range of values. If you multiply or divide by a negative, flip the sign.

Example3x − 2 > 7
3x > 9, so x > 3

Simultaneous

Simultaneous equations are two (or more) equations solved together. The answer works in all of them at the same time.

Examplex + y = 10 and x − y = 4
Answer: x = 7, y = 3

Quadratic

The highest power of the variable is 2. A quadratic equation has the form ax² + bx + c = 0, where a ≠ 0.

Examplex² − 5x + 6 = 0

Quadratic formula

A formula that solves any quadratic equation ax² + bx + c = 0:
x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) ÷ 2a

Examplex² + 4x + 1 = 0: a = 1, b = 4, c = 1
x = −2 ± √3, so x ≈ −0.27 or −3.73

Roots

The solutions of an equation. On a graph, the roots are where the curve crosses the x-axis.

Examplex² − 5x + 6 = 0 has roots x = 2 and x = 3.
