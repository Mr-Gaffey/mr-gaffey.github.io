Complex number

A number of the form a + bi, where a and b are real and i = √−1 (so i² = −1).

Example3 + 4i and −2 − i

Imaginary number

A number that is a multiple of i, where i = √−1. It has no real part.

Example3i and −5i are imaginary numbers. √−16 = 4i.

Argand diagram

A diagram for plotting complex numbers. Real part on the horizontal axis, imaginary part on the vertical axis.

Example2 + 3i is plotted at the point (2, 3).

Real part

The a in a + bi.

ExampleThe real part of 3 − 5i is 3.

Imaginary part

The b in a + bi (the number in front of i, without the i).

ExampleThe imaginary part of 3 − 5i is −5.

Translation

Adding a complex number moves (translates) a point on the Argand diagram: every point moves the same distance in the same direction.

ExampleAdding 2 + i to 1 + 3i moves it 2 right and 1 up, to 3 + 4i.

Dilation

Multiplying a complex number by a real number k stretches (k > 1) or shrinks (0 < k < 1) its distance from 0, in the same direction.

Example2(1 + 3i) = 2 + 6i is twice as far from 0.

Modulus

The distance from 0 to the complex number on an Argand diagram. |a + bi| = √(a² + b²)

Example|3 + 4i| = √(9 + 16) = 5

Conjugate

The same complex number with the sign of the imaginary part changed. The conjugate of a + bi is a − bi.

ExampleThe conjugate of 2 + 7i is 2 − 7i.

Rotation

Multiplying a complex number by i rotates it 90° anticlockwise about 0 on the Argand diagram.

Examplei(3 + 2i) = 3i + 2i² = −2 + 3i
