Sample space

The set (list) of all possible outcomes.

ExampleRolling a die: {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}

Systematic listing

Listing outcomes in an organised order so you don't miss any.

ExampleTossing a coin twice: HH, HT, TH, TT

Two-way table

A grid that shows all the outcomes of two events together.

ExampleTwo dice in a 6 × 6 grid give 36 outcomes.

Tree diagram

A diagram with branches showing the outcomes of one event followed by another.

ExampleA coin tossed twice has 4 branch endings.

Fundamental Principle of Counting

If one thing can be done in m ways and another in n ways, both together can be done in m × n ways.

Example3 starters and 4 mains: 3 × 4 = 12 different meals

Arrangements

A way of putting objects in order, where the order matters.

ExampleA, B, C can be arranged in 3! = 6 ways.
1st, 2nd, 3rd from 10 runners: 10 × 9 × 8 = 720

Permutations

Another word for arrangements: the number of ways of putting objects in order, where order matters. Choosing r objects from n in order gives n × (n − 1) × … (r numbers multiplied).

Example1st, 2nd and 3rd from 10 runners: 10 × 9 × 8 = 720 permutations.

Factorial

n! means multiply all the whole numbers from n down to 1. It counts the ways to arrange n different objects.

Example5! = 5 × 4 × 3 × 2 × 1 = 120. (0! = 1)
